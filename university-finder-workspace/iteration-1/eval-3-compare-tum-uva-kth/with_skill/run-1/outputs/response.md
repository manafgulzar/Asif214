# TU Munich vs University of Amsterdam vs KTH: MSc Mechanical Engineering (Indian applicant, 8.4 CGPA, GRE 318, ₹12–14 lakh/yr)

**Your profile:** Indian, BE/BTech (assumed in mechanical or a close field) from a tier-2 college, CGPA 8.4/10, GRE 318, no IELTS yet. Budget ₹12–14 lakh a year for tuition plus living.
**Assumptions:** You're aiming for the **2027 autumn intake**. The 2026 deadlines have already passed. You don't have much full-time work experience, and you'll need a loan or family money for anything above your budget. Exchange rates used: **€1 ≈ ₹100, SEK 1 ≈ ₹9**. These are approximate, so recheck them before you plan.
**Grade conversion (approximate):** 8.4/10 works out to about **German 1.8–2.0** on the modified Bavarian formula. The exact figure depends on the minimum passing grade the university uses for your college. In US terms that's roughly a **3.4–3.6 GPA**. That's a solid profile, but it's not a standout one for the most competitive programs.

---

## Short answer

1. **The University of Amsterdam (UvA) is out.** UvA doesn't run an MSc in Mechanical Engineering. It's a general research university with no mechanical engineering faculty. In the Netherlands, mechanical engineering is taught at TU Delft, TU Eindhoven, the University of Twente and Groningen. Please confirm this on uva.nl, but I found no such program. The Dutch technical universities also charge non-EU students around €20–26k a year in tuition alone (TU Delft is about **€25.6k for 2026–27**). With living costs, that's roughly **₹35–40 lakh a year**, which is about three times your budget.
2. **TU Munich is the best of your three, but it's over budget as things stand.** Since 2024 TUM charges non-EU master's students **€4,000–6,000 per semester**. Add Munich living costs and you get about **₹21–29 lakh a year**. It's realistic only if you (a) work as a student (*Werkstudent*) part-time, which is legal and common in Germany, (b) win a fee waiver or scholarship, or (c) accept a loan. For admission it's a **Reach**. TUM chooses students through a points-based aptitude assessment, and a tier-2 8.4 puts you in the competitive zone, not the safe one.
3. **KTH is a good academic fit but the most out of reach on cost.** Tuition is about **SEK 342,000 for the full 2-year program** (about SEK 171k a year). Add Stockholm living costs and you get about **₹27–30 lakh a year**. It only works if you win the **KTH Scholarship**, which covers tuition and is highly competitive. Apply by **15 January 2027** and apply for the scholarship at the same time. Without it, KTH doesn't fit your budget.

**My recommendation:** of your three, apply to **TUM**, and to **KTH only together with its scholarship**. Drop UvA. More importantly, add **tuition-free German technical universities and the Italian Politecnicos**. Those are the options that actually fit ₹12–14 lakh a year, and some of them are just as good or better for mechanical engineering.

## Shortlist

### 🎯 Reach
- **Technical University of Munich (TUM)** (Germany): M.Sc. Mechanical Engineering, Garching campus. It's one of Europe's top mechanical engineering schools, with very strong links to the automotive and aerospace industry in Bavaria. The main hurdles are the aptitude assessment (your bachelor's coursework has to cover their core subjects: higher maths, technical mechanics, machine elements, materials, thermodynamics) and the cost of about ₹21–29 lakh a year. Check which tracks can be completed fully in English. TUM's page lists "English or German" proficiency, but some electives are taught in German.
- **KTH Royal Institute of Technology** (Sweden): MSc Machine Design / Engineering Design. It's a strong program with good industry projects. Your grades are around the competitive range, so admission is plausible. The **budget is the reach**, because it only fits with the KTH Scholarship.

### ✅ Match
- **RWTH Aachen** (Germany): English-taught mechanical engineering master's programs (for example Automotive Engineering, or Computer Aided Conception and Production in Mechanical Engineering). RWTH matches TUM's reputation in mechanical engineering. Most programs charge **no tuition, only a semester fee of about €300**, so the total is about **₹12–14 lakh a year**, which fits your budget. Some "international" programs do charge fees, so check each program.
- **Hamburg University of Technology (TUHH)** (Germany): MSc Mechanical Engineering & Management, or MSc Mechatronics (English). No tuition, a semester fee of about €350, and a good industry base around Hamburg (Airbus, shipping). Total is about **₹12–14 lakh a year**.
- **Politecnico di Milano** (Italy): MSc Mechanical Engineering (English). It's ranked in the top tier in Europe for mechanical engineering. Non-EU tuition depends on family income, with a maximum of about €3,900 a year. The **DSU regional scholarship** can waive fees and pay a living stipend. Total is about **₹13–16 lakh a year** without the scholarship and well under budget with it. Applications run in rounds from about Nov to Mar, so check the dates.

### 🛟 Safety
- **Politecnico di Torino** (Italy): MSc Mechanical Engineering / Automotive Engineering (English). Its admission bar is lower than Milan's, Turin is cheaper to live in, and it offers income-based fees plus the EDISU regional scholarship. Total is about **₹11–14 lakh a year**.
- **A less-crowded German public university**, such as TU Chemnitz or Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg: these have English-taught mechanical, automotive and manufacturing master's programs with no tuition, and living costs are lower than in Munich (about ₹11–12 lakh a year). Check each program's language and curriculum fit. I haven't verified individual programs here.

> There's no honest "safety" among your original three. TUM is selective, KTH depends on a scholarship, and UvA doesn't offer the degree.

## Funding options
- **KTH Scholarship**: covers tuition for selected students admitted to KTH master's programs. You apply separately; the window is expected around Dec 2026 to 15 Jan 2027 (verify). Without it, drop KTH.
- **Working as a student in Germany (*Werkstudent*)**: non-EU students can legally work 140 full days or 280 half days a year. Many mechanical engineering students earn €800–1,200 a month at companies near TUM or RWTH. This is the most realistic way to make TUM affordable, but you can't count it toward the blocked-account requirement.
- **Italian regional scholarships (DSU Lombardia / EDISU Piemonte)**: based on family income, and you need an ISEE-equivalent income certificate from India. They can mean free tuition, a stipend and sometimes housing. This is the strongest route to studying almost free.
- **Deutschlandstipendium**: €300 a month, merit-based, offered by most German universities after you enrol.
- **Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's**: fully funded (tuition plus about €1,400 a month). Search the EMJM catalogue for mechanical engineering, materials, robotics or energy programs. Most deadlines are Dec–Feb.
- *(DAAD EPOS and the Swedish Institute's SISGP usually require 2+ years or 3,000+ hours of work experience. They're probably not an option for you now unless you have that experience.)*

## Next steps
1. **This week: book IELTS Academic** for a date in **November 2026**, targeting **7.0 overall with no band below 6.5**. That clears every program here and leaves time for one retake before KTH's 15 Jan deadline.
2. **Now: apply for your APS certificate (APS India).** It's required for German universities and the student visa, and it can take 1–3 months. You need it before TUM opens in April 2027 and before RWTH and TUHH deadlines.
3. **By 15 Nov 2026: check your coursework against each program.** Map your transcript to TUM's required core subjects (higher maths, technical mechanics, machine elements, materials science, thermodynamics). German universities admit or reject partly on this, more than on GRE.
4. **16 Oct 2026 – 15 Jan 2027: apply to KTH on universityadmissions.se** (SEK 900 fee) **and** apply for the KTH Scholarship. Upload documents by about 1 Feb. Results come out 1 Apr 2027. Only enrol if you get the scholarship.
5. **Nov 2026 – Mar 2027: apply to Politecnico di Milano and Torino** in their early rounds, then apply for DSU/EDISU as soon as you're admitted. Prepare your family-income documents early.
6. **1 Apr – 31 May 2027: apply to TUM** (M.Sc. Mechanical Engineering, through TUMonline), and in the same spring window to **RWTH Aachen and TU Hamburg** (check each deadline; some are earlier than TUM's).
7. **Documents to start now:** an SOP tailored to each program (name the specialisation, for example automotive, robotics or thermo-fluids, and the professors or labs you'd like to work with), 2 recommendation letters from professors, a CV listing projects and internships, and your transcripts plus degree certificate (provisional is fine).
8. **About GRE 318:** TUM and KTH don't officially ask for it. Include it where it's optional, especially if your Quant score is 165 or higher, because it helps balance the tier-2 college. Don't spend more time retaking it.
