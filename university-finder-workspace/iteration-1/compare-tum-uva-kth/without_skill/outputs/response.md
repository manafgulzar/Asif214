# TU Munich vs University of Amsterdam vs KTH: MS in Mechanical Engineering

**Short answer:** Of the three, **TU Munich is the best choice**. But two things change the picture:

1. **The University of Amsterdam (UvA) doesn't offer a master's in mechanical engineering.** UvA is a general research university with no mechanical engineering faculty. In the Netherlands, the ME master's degrees are at **TU Delft, TU Eindhoven and the University of Twente**. Take UvA off your list. If you want the Netherlands, compare against those three instead.
2. **At about ₹12–14 lakh a year, none of these options is fully affordable without a scholarship, a fee waiver or part-time work.** TUM comes closest. Also look at **tuition-free German universities** like RWTH Aachen, TU Dresden, TU Berlin, TU Darmstadt, TU Braunschweig and Leibniz Uni Hannover. They fit your budget almost exactly and have strong ME departments.

## 3. Recommendation

### Pick TU Munich over KTH, for these reasons

- **ROI:** Germany's automotive, aerospace and machinery industries make it the best place in Europe to start an ME career. Munich and Bavaria sit at the centre of that.
- **Cost:** It costs about ₹3–5 L less per year than KTH, and German working-student jobs can realistically bring in ₹6–10 L per year once you settle in.
- **Fit:** Your 8.4 CGPA converts to a grade that matters directly in TUM's assessment. KTH's preference for well-known universities works against a tier-2 college.

**Caveat:** Before you apply, check how many TUM ME core modules are taught only in German. Some electives are. The English-taught alternatives (M.Sc. Mechatronics & Robotics, Aerospace) avoid this problem.

### Strongly consider tuition-free German universities (they fit your budget)

Public universities outside Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg still charge **no tuition**, only a semester fee of about €300–350. Your yearly cost is then mostly living expenses: about **€11,000–12,000 ≈ ₹11.5–13 L**, which is **inside your budget**.

|---|---|---|

| TU Dresden | M.Sc. Mechanical Engineering (some taught in German), Advanced Computational & Civil Eng. | Lower cost of living |
| TU Darmstadt | M.Sc. Mechanical & Process Engineering | Strong industry links |

### A suggested shortlist (apply to 5–7 schools)
- **Reach:** TU Munich, RWTH Aachen, (KTH if you're applying for the KTH Scholarship)
- **Target:** TU Darmstadt, TU Dresden, TU Hamburg, University of Stuttgart
- **Safer:** TU Braunschweig, Leibniz Uni Hannover, TU Chemnitz, Otto-von-Guericke Magdeburg

## 4. Next steps and deadlines (for the Winter 2027 intake)

1. **Now (Oct–Nov 2026):** Apply for the **APS certificate**. Book **IELTS**, aiming for 7.0.
2. **Start learning German now** (Goethe A1 → A2 by arrival). This matters a lot for part-time jobs and full-time ME jobs in Germany.
3. **KTH:** Applications on universityadmissions.se usually open mid-October and close **mid-January**. The KTH Scholarship has the same deadline and needs the SEK 900 application fee paid.
4. **TUM:** The winter-semester application window usually runs **about Dec/Jan to 31 May** (check the programme page). Prepare a curriculum/module description, a CV and a strong motivation letter (TUM scores it).
5. **Other German universities:** Most winter deadlines fall between 15 Jan and 15 July (some go through uni-assist). RWTH ME is usually 1 March for non-EU applicants.
6. **Finances:** Plan for a blocked account of about €11,900 (it will cover your living costs). Look into TUM fee waivers, the **DAAD** scholarships (most need work experience), **Deutschlandstipendium** (€300 a month, applied for after admission) and education loans from Indian banks or NBFCs for the remaining gap.

---

## Bottom line

- **TU Munich vs KTH:** Choose TUM. It has the better ME reputation, stronger industry access, lower total cost and better odds for your profile.
- **UvA:** It doesn't offer ME. Look at TU Delft, TU/e or Twente instead, but they cost far more than your budget.
- **To stay within ₹12–14 L,** add tuition-free German universities (RWTH Aachen, TU Darmstadt, TU Dresden, TUHH) next to TUM. Germany fits your budget and career goals best.
