# University Shortlist: Grade 12, Toronto, 91% average, Nursing (BScN), Fall 2027

**Your daughter's profile:** Ontario high school student (OUAC 101 applicant), about 91% average, wants a 4-year Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BScN) starting Fall 2027. Ontario preferred, other provinces acceptable. Budget is flexible. She wants strong clinical placement or co-op.

**Assumptions:**
- The 91% is her current average. Nursing schools rank applicants on the **top six Grade 12 U/M courses, including the prerequisites**. She will need **ENG4U, SBI4U (Biology), SCH4U (Chemistry) and a Grade 12 U math**. If any of these are missing from her timetable, fix that first. Her Biology and Chemistry marks will matter more than her overall average.
- She is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, so she pays domestic fees.
- She is applying straight from high school, so she needs direct-entry programs. That rules out **U of T's Bloomberg Faculty of Nursing**, which is a 2-year *second-entry* program. Applicants need at least 2 years of university first. One possible route is a science degree somewhere first and then Bloomberg, but it takes longer.

### First, about "co-op" in nursing
Almost no Canadian BScN program offers co-op in the engineering or business sense. Nursing does things differently: **supervised clinical placements are a required part of every accredited BScN in Canada.** They are needed to write the NCLEX-RN licensing exam. Students usually start in hospitals, long-term care or community health in year 1 or 2 and finish with a full-time "consolidation" placement in the final year. So when you compare schools, look at:
1. Which hospitals the school partners with, and whether she can do placements close to home.
2. How early clinical work starts.
3. The final-year consolidation placement. Many students get hired from it.

Paid hands-on experience also exists outside co-op. Many Ontario hospitals hire nursing students in the summer as "nursing student externs" or "unregulated care providers," usually after year 2 or 3. Ask about this at open houses.

## Shortlist

### Reach (very competitive even at 91%)
- **McMaster University** (Hamilton, ON). BScN (Nursing I, OUAC code MN). This is one of Canada's best-known nursing schools, with small-group problem-based learning and placements across the Hamilton hospital network. **The main hurdle:** admission is 80% grades and 20% **Casper** (an online situational-judgment test). The minimum to be considered is 85%, and offers usually go much higher. A strong Casper score can carry a 91% student.
- **Queen's University** (Kingston, ON). Bachelor of Nursing Science (BNSc). It has a small, close-knit class, clinical work at Kingston teaching hospitals, and a strong reputation. **The main hurdle:** Queen's says its admitted average across all programs is about 90.5%, and nursing is among its most competitive. She also has to complete a supplementary application.
- **Toronto Metropolitan University (TMU)** (downtown Toronto). 4-year Collaborative BScN with Centennial and George Brown. Students admitted to the TMU site do all four years at TMU. She could live at home and do placements in Toronto hospitals. **The main hurdle:** TMU lists its grade range as **"low 90s"**, so a 91% is right on the line.

### Match (her 91% is at or above the typical range)
- **Western University** (London, ON). The new **Direct Entry 3.5-year BScN** (OUAC code ENW), with its first class in Sept 2026. Graduating a semester early means starting work sooner. Clinical placements run through London's teaching hospitals. **The main hurdle:** **Casper is required**, and the guideline is "high 80s to low 90s." Western gives no fixed cutoff. Because the program is new, there is little admissions history to go on.
- **York University** (Toronto). Direct Entry BScN. It has a strong community and clinical program, and she could commute from home. York says the average must be "at least the high 80s," with 70%+ in each required course. Self-reported data suggests admitted students have a median around 91%. Treat York as a solid match, not a sure thing.
- **University of Ottawa** (Ottawa, ON). BScN (English or French). It has a large program with broad hospital partnerships, and bilingual nursing is a career asset. I could not confirm a current cutoff from an official source. Mid-to-high 80s is the usual expectation, so check the website.

### Safety (comfortably above the typical range)
- **Ontario Tech University** (Oshawa, ON). BScN. It is commutable from east Toronto and has modern simulation labs. Third-party data puts the competitive average around the low-to-mid 80s, so I could not confirm an official figure. Her 91% should be comfortably above it.
- **Nipissing University** (North Bay, ON). BScN. The official listing gives a minimum of 70% and says **the 2026 admission average was "mid 80s"**. The program is small (96 seats), with strong rural and northern placements. She is well above the range, and Nipissing also has a later deadline.

### Outside Ontario (optional)
- **Dalhousie University** (Halifax, NS). BScN. This is a **reach for an Ontario student, whatever her marks**: the program mainly serves Nova Scotia residents and keeps only "a few" direct-entry seats for applicants from other provinces. Casper is required.
- **University of Alberta** (Edmonton, AB). BScN Collaborative. It is a large, highly regarded faculty that takes high school students directly. Offers come out in rounds. The listed application deadline is **March 1**, but check this for 2027. Casper is required.
- **McGill University** (Montréal, QC). BScN. It has a strong reputation, but students from outside Quebec usually start with an extra foundation year, so the program takes 4+ years. Tuition for out-of-province Canadians went up sharply in 2024. The deadline is not confirmed, so check the website.

## Funding options
1. **Automatic entrance scholarships.** Most Ontario universities give these to students with roughly 90%+ averages. Amounts vary by school, and no separate application is usually needed. Check each school's scholarship page for current amounts.
2. **Major / competitive entrance awards** (e.g., Queen's major admission awards, McMaster and Western named awards). These need separate applications, often due **earlier than the program deadline** (some in late fall), so check them now.
3. **OSAP.** Ontario's student aid. It is income-tested, so even families who don't expect much should check eligibility once she accepts an offer.
4. **Hospital foundation and nursing association bursaries** in upper years, plus Ontario's **Nursing Graduate Guarantee**, which funds new-grad nursing positions after graduation.

## Next steps (today is Oct 10, 2026)
1. **This week:** Check that her Grade 12 timetable includes **ENG4U, SBI4U, SCH4U and a 4U math (MHF4U, MCV4U or MDM4U)**. Push hard on Bio and Chem. Many schools rank applicants on these marks, and first-semester midterms go out to universities.
2. **October–November:** Get her OUAC 101 login (PIN) from guidance and start the application. Attend the **Ontario Universities' Fair / virtual open houses**, and at each nursing school ask: *"Where do first- and second-year clinical placements happen, and how are final consolidation placements assigned?"*
3. **By early December:** Choose 5–7 programs, for example: McMaster, Queen's, TMU, Western, York, Ontario Tech, Nipissing. Every pick here is in Ontario, so they all fit in one OUAC application. Each extra choice beyond the base 3 costs extra. Look up deadlines for **major entrance scholarships**, which can fall before the program deadline.
4. **Casper prep and booking (now through January):** McMaster, Western, Dalhousie and Alberta all require **Casper**. One sitting can usually be sent to several schools. **Book a test date as soon as the 2026-27 dates are posted at takeCasper.com.** Seats fill up, and missing Casper means automatic rejection at those schools.
5. **Submit OUAC by January 15, 2027.** This is the equal-consideration deadline for Ontario high school students and the firm deadline for McMaster. Aim for mid-December so you aren't rushing.
6. **Queen's:** Application by **Feb 1, 2027**, and supplementary application by **Feb 15, 2027**.
7. **Nipissing:** Equal-consideration deadline **Feb 17, 2027**. It is covered if you have already applied by Jan 15.
8. **Out-of-province (optional):** Apply directly on each school's own portal. U of Alberta's listed deadline is **March 1** (check for 2027). Dalhousie and McGill are not confirmed, so check their sites in November.
9. **Spring 2027:** Ontario universities must give decisions to Jan-15 applicants by about late May. She can't be required to accept an offer before **June 1, 2027**.

**Honest summary:** At 91% she is competitive everywhere on this list but guaranteed nowhere at the top. Nursing is one of the most oversubscribed programs in Ontario. A balanced list such as McMaster, Queen's and TMU as reaches, Western, York and uOttawa as matches, and Ontario Tech and Nipissing as safeties gives her a very high chance of starting a BScN in Fall 2027. Her best levers are **strong Bio and Chem marks** and **good Casper preparation**.
